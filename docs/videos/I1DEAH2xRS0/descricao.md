The Alaska megatsunami ran 1,578 feet up a fjord wall at 5:26 in the morning. Half a million people sail into that fjord every summer - here is how close the tour boats came to being in it.

On August 10, 2025, a mountainside above the South Sawyer Glacier let go into Tracy Arm, a narrow fjord south of Juneau that cruise ships and tour boats visit on a schedule. Roughly 64 million cubic meters of rock hit the water at once. The wave it threw ran 481 meters - 1,578 feet - up the opposite slope, the second-highest wave ever measured anywhere. The tour boats had not arrived yet.

Here is what the field teams found. U.S.G.S. crews mapped trim lines between 470 and 500 meters, and the tide gauge in Juneau, 80 miles away, still registered 14 inches. The record itself belongs to Lituya Bay on July ninth, 1958, when the Fairweather Fault dropped 30 million cubic meters into Gilbert Inlet and pushed water 1,719 feet up the far ridge. And above the town of Whittier, the Barry Arm slope is still moving - though the U.S.G.S. revised its own modeled wave at the town from about 30 feet down to roughly 7 feet in Open-File Report 2021-1071. That correction matters as much as the number it replaced. This is not a prediction that a failure is imminent.
