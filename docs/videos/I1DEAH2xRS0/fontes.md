### Texto integral do documento, incluindo suas referências

Sources - "1,719 Feet: The Tallest Wave in Recorded History (Alaska)"
Critical Latitude. This video is educational. It is not a prediction that any slope is about to fail. Every figure stated on screen is traceable to one of the sources below.
The August 10, 2025 Tracy Arm event
U.S. Geological Survey, Landslide Hazards Program - the official event page for the 2025 Tracy Arm landslide-generated tsunami. Source for the date and time (August 10, 2025, 05:26 AKDT / 13:26 UTC), the failure above the toe of the South Sawyer Glacier, the ongoing rockfall hazard at the scar, and the statement that no injuries or fatalities were reported.
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/landslide-hazards/science/2025-tracy-arm-landslide-generated-tsunami
U.S. Geological Survey - the 2025 Tracy Arm landslide overview map, used for the geography of the slide path, the fjord, and Sawyer Island.
https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/2025-tracy-arm-landslide-overview-map
Shugar et al., Science - "A 481-meter-high landslide-tsunami in a cruise ship-frequented Alaska fjord." The peer-reviewed source for the 481 meter (1,578 foot) maximum runup, the landslide volume, the trim-line mapping, and the cruise-traffic context.
https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec3187
Alaska Earthquake Center, University of Alaska Fairbanks - "Major landslide in southeast Alaska fjord." Source for the seismic signal generated by the slide and its magnitude-equivalent, and for the independent timing of the event.
https://earthquake.alaska.edu/major-landslide-southeast-alaska-fjord
NASA Earth Observatory - "Tracy Arm's Post-Tsunami Landscape." Source for the satellite before-and-after imagery, the roughly 64 million cubic meters of displaced rock, and the observation that water continued to slosh in the fjord for more than a day.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/tracy-arms-post-tsunami-landscape/
Eos (American Geophysical Union) - "The Forensics of a Skyscraper-Sized Tsunami." Background reporting on how the runup was reconstructed and on the role of glacier retreat in destabilizing the slope.
https://eos.org/articles/the-forensics-of-a-skyscraper-sized-tsunami
Eos, The Landslide Blog (Dave Petley) - "The 10 August 2025 landslide and tsunami at Tracy Arm fjord in Alaska." Source for the subaerial volume estimate and the height of the landslide crown above the fjord.
https://eos.org/thelandslideblog/the-10-august-2025-landslide-and-tsunami-at-tracy-arm-fjord-in-alaska
University of Calgary - "Huge tsunami in popular area for Alaskan cruises provides lessons for steep, mountainous terrain." Researcher commentary on what the event means for other fjords with the same geometry.
https://ucalgary.ca/news/huge-tsunami-popular-area-alaskan-cruises-provides-lessons-steep-mountainous-terrain
Lituya Bay, July 9, 1958 - the record
Miller, D.J., 1960, "Giant Waves in Lituya Bay, Alaska": U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 354-C. The original field investigation and the source of the 1,720-foot figure (rounded to 1,719 feet in most modern accounts), the Fairweather Fault trigger, the roughly 30 million cubic meters that fell into Gilbert Inlet, and the eyewitness accounts from the boats in the bay.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp354C
Barry Arm and the Whittier hazard
Barnhart, K.R., Jones, R.P., George, D.L., Coe, J.A., and Staley, D.M., 2021, "Preliminary assessment of the wave generating potential from landslides at Barry Arm, Prince William Sound, Alaska": U.S. Geological Survey Open-File Report 2021-1071. The modeling report that revised the wave height at Whittier down to roughly 2 meters (about 7 feet) and gives the under-20-minute travel time.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20211071
U.S. Geological Survey - "Potential Landslide-Generated Tsunami in Prince William Sound's Barry Arm Likely Less Severe than Previously Thought." The agency's own plain-language statement of the correction from the earlier 30-foot estimate.
https://www.usgs.gov/news/state-news-release/potential-landslide-generated-tsunami-prince-william-sounds-barry-arm
U.S. Geological Survey, Landslide Hazards Program - "Barry Arm, Alaska, Tsunami Hazard Maybe Not So Severe." Companion explainer for the revised assessment and its remaining caveats for marine traffic.
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/landslide-hazards/science/barry-arm-alaska-tsunami-hazard-maybe-not-so-severe
U.S. Geological Survey Fact Sheet 2022-3020 - the Barry Arm landslide fact sheet, source for the slope's size, the monitoring rationale, and the summary hazard framing.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20223020
U.S. Geological Survey - "Monitoring Efforts at the Barry Arm Landslide." Source for what instruments are actually on the slope right now and what they measure.
https://www.usgs.gov/tools/monitoring-efforts-barry-arm-landslide
Alaska Division of Geological and Geophysical Surveys - "Barry Arm landslide and tsunami hazard." The state hazard page, source for the state-level monitoring and public information effort.
https://dggs.alaska.gov/hazards/barry-arm-landslide.html
Alaska Public Media - "Researchers revisit Barry Arm tsunami threat." Reporting on the field campaigns that followed the initial 2020 alarm.
https://alaskapublic.org/2021/07/13/researchers-revisit-barry-arm-tsunami-threat/
Glacier retreat and slope stability
Dai, C., et al., 2020, "Detection and assessment of a large and potentially tsunamigenic periglacial landslide in Barry Arm, Alaska." The paper behind the mechanism described in the video: retreating glaciers stop buttressing lower slopes, and unsupported valley walls deform.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7816221/
Klean Industries - "Alaska landslides, megatsunamis, glacier retreat and climate risk." Background synthesis on the regional pattern of glacier-linked slope failures.
https://kleanindustries.com/insights/market-analysis-reports/alaska-landslides-megatsunamis-glacier-retreat-climate-risk/
