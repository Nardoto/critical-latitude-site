Sources - California's Supervolcano Is Cooling Down
Critical Latitude. This video is educational and is not a prediction that an eruption or earthquake is imminent.
The 2023 imaging study
Biondi, Zhu, Li, Williams and Zhan, "An upper-crust lid over the Long Valley magma chamber," Science Advances, October 18, 2023. The distributed acoustic sensing survey, the 100 km of fiber optic cable, the crystallized lid, and the magma chamber at roughly 12 km depth.
https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adi9878
Open-access full text of the same paper, for readers without a Science subscription.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10584340/
Caltech Division of Geological and Planetary Sciences news release, "California Supervolcano is Cooling Off but May Still Cause Quakes." Source of the Zhongwen Zhan quote used in the video: the region is not gearing up for another supervolcanic eruption, but cooling may release enough gas and liquid to cause earthquakes and small eruptions.
https://www.gps.caltech.edu/news-and-events/news/california-supervolcano-is-cooling-off-but-may-still-cause-quakes
Current monitoring and alert level
California Volcano Observatory, "Is it just me, or is it quiet? A seismically uneventful year for Long Valley," January 2026. Source of the figure that only six earthquakes of magnitude 1.0 or larger were located inside the Long Valley Caldera box in all of 2025, the first year since 1980 with fewer than ten.
https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/calvo/news/it-just-me-or-it-quiet-a-seismically-uneventful-year-long-valley
USGS Long Valley Caldera overview. The caldera dimensions, the 760,000-year age, the Bishop Tuff, the resurgent dome, and the current alert level.
https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/long-valley-caldera
USGS Mono-Inyo Craters. The chain of domes and vents north and west of the caldera, and the migration of activity out of the caldera over the last 100,000 years.
https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mono-inyo-craters
Eruption likelihood
USGS Fact Sheet 108-96, "Future Eruptions in California's Long Valley Area - What's Likely?" Source of the annual probability language, the statement that small to moderate eruptions have occurred somewhere along the Mono-Inyo volcanic chain every few hundred years over the past 4,000 years, and the statement that the chances of an eruption in the area in any given year are quite small.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/fs108-96/fs108-96.pdf
USGS Bulletin 1933, "Response Plan for Volcano Hazards in the Long Valley Caldera and Mono Craters Region, California." The hazard framework and the alert-level response structure.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b2185/home.html
USGS Bulletin 1933 full report PDF.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1933/report.pdf
The carbon dioxide at Mammoth Mountain
USGS, "Carbon Dioxide at Mammoth Mountain." Source of the 170 acres of tree kill across six general areas, the 20 to 90 percent carbon dioxide in soil gas inside the kill zones against less than 1 percent outside, and the continuous year-round monitoring at four sites.
https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mammoth-mountain/science/carbon-dioxide-mammoth-mountain
USGS Fact Sheet 172-96, "Invisible CO2 Gas Killing Trees at Mammoth Mountain, California." The 1989 earthquake swarm, the 1990 discovery of tree kill by U.S. Forest Service rangers, and the 1994 soil-gas survey that identified the cause.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/fs172-96/fs172-96.pdf
USGS Long Valley Caldera field guide, Horseshoe Lake. The most visible tree-kill area, the trailhead, and the fencing and signage at the site.
https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/long-valley-caldera/science/long-valley-caldera-field-guide-horseshoe-lake
USGS volcanic gas monitoring at Mammoth Mountain. The gas instrumentation that has run on the mountain since 1990.
https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mammoth-mountain/science/volcanic-gas-monitoring-mammoth-mountain
The 2006 ski patrol deaths
Cal/OSHA citation coverage of the April 6, 2006 deaths of three Mammoth Mountain ski patrollers. James Juarez and John Scott McAndrews fell roughly 20 feet through the snowpack into a volcanic fumarole; Charles Rosenthal died attempting the rescue. All three died of oxygen deprivation caused by carbon dioxide.
https://www.ehstoday.com/standards/osha/article/21908419/deaths-of-three-ski-patrollers-prompt-cal-osha-citations
Contemporaneous news reporting confirming the coroner's finding that volcanic gas killed the patrollers.
https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna12194801
The 1980 earthquakes and the 1982 warning
1980 Mammoth Lakes earthquakes. Four events of roughly magnitude 6 beginning May 25, 1980, the largest at Mw 6.2, with damage in Mammoth Lakes.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Mammoth_Lakes_earthquakes
Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program report on Long Valley, May 1982. Documents the USGS Notice of Potential Volcanic Hazard issued May 25, 1982, alongside the earthquake hazard watch in effect since May 1980.
https://volcano.si.edu/showreport.cfm?doi=10.5479/si.GVP.SEAN198205-323822
USGS, "Volcanic unrest and hazard communication in Long Valley Volcanic Region, California." The scientific community's own assessment of the 1982 notice, the community and media reaction, and the communication failure it became a case study for.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70189236
USGS Bulletin 2185, Appendix A, "History of Volcanism in the Long Valley Region." The 1982 notice, the Casa Diablo fumaroles discovered in January 1982, and the 25 cm of resurgent dome uplift that prompted it.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b2185/appenda.html
