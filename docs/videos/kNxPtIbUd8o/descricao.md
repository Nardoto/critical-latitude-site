Two tsunami evacuation towers exist in Washington. The state's own assessment says its coast needs at least 58, and possibly more than 80. Here is what the county tables actually show - and why Oregon spent the same decade moving in the opposite direction.

If you are standing in Westport when Cascadia moves, the shaking runs for minutes and the first wave arrives 10 to 15 minutes after it stops. Washington's Emergency Management Division has measured how long it takes to walk out of the inundation zone from parts of the outer coast, and in many places the answer is an hour or more. Ocosta Elementary School opened in 2016 as the first vertical evacuation structure in North America, a gym roof rated for over 1,000 people on piles driven 50 feet past the soil expected to liquefy. The Shoalwater Bay Indian Tribe dedicated the first freestanding tower at Tokeland on August fifth, 2022, and opened it to anyone in the area. A third is funded and due to break ground in Westport's Marina District at around $11.4 million, on a $16.7 million FEMA grant awarded in November 2023. Across the Columbia River, Oregon banned new schools and hospitals inside the inundation zone in 1995, then repealed that ban in 2019 with House Bill 3309. Same fault. Same wave. Opposite directions.
