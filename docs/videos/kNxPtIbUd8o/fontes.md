Sources — When Cascadia Hits, This Coast Has 15 Minutes
Educational commentary, not a prediction of an imminent earthquake. Every figure in the video traces to one of the sources below.
Washington — the program and the need
Washington Emergency Management Division, tsunami hazards. The state's overview of tsunami risk and vertical evacuation.
https://mil.wa.gov/emergency-management-division/hazards/tsunami
WA EMD, outer coast tsunami vertical evacuation assessment completed, November 4 2021. Source for the statewide need of at least 58 and potentially more than 80 structures, and for the county breakdown: Pacific 25-40, Grays Harbor 30-42, Clallam 1-3.
https://mil.wa.gov/news/outer-coast-tsunami-vertical-evacuation-assessment-completed
Manual for Tsunami Vertical Evacuation Structures, 2nd Edition, 2024. The state design reference. Source for the tsunami load set named in the video: hydrodynamic force, buoyancy, debris impact, debris damming and scour.
https://mil.wa.gov/asset/6723ccad3ed27/Manual%20for%20Tsunami%20Vertical%20Evacuation%20Structures%202nd%20Edition%202024.pdf
Ocosta Elementary School, Westport — the first structure, 2016
WA EMD, nation's first tsunami vertical evacuation center breaks ground.
https://mil.wa.gov/news/nations-first-tsunami-vertical-evacuation-center-breaks-ground
Degenkolb Engineers, structural engineer of record. Source for the refuge platform at roughly 55 feet above sea level and 28 feet above grade, capacity over 1,000, piles driven about 50 feet past liquefiable soil, and the performance-based design approach.
https://degenkolb.com/work/ocosta-elementary-school-tsunami-vertical-evacuation-building/
Shoalwater Bay Indian Tribe tower, Tokeland — dedicated August 5 2022
WA EMD, celebrating the nation's first tsunami vertical evacuation tower. Source for the dedication date, the capacity of more than 400, the $1.2 million tribal contribution and the FEMA contribution of more than $3 million, and for the quotes from Chairwoman Charlene Nelson, Lee Shipman, Lynn Clark and WA EMD director Robert Ezelle.
https://mil.wa.gov/news/celebrating-nations-first-tsunami-vertical-evacuation-tower
Seattle Times, new coastal Washington tsunami tower offers residents a place to escape.
https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/new-coastal-washington-tsumani-tower-offers-residents-a-place-to-escape/
OPB, Shoalwater Bay Indian Tribe earthquake and tsunami tower, August 8 2022. Source for the 15 to 20 minute walk time and the roughly 35 minute wave arrival window at Tokeland.
https://www.opb.org/article/2022/08/08/coastal-washington-shoalwater-bay-indian-tribe-earthquake-tsunami/
Westport Marina District tower — under construction
FEMA, Westport vertical evacuation project. Source for the $16.7 million grant awarded November 2023 and the 2,000-person capacity.
https://www.fema.gov/node/465491
The Daily World, Westport gets bird's-eye view of tsunami tower plans, June 1 2026. Source for the roughly $11.4 million construction cost, the late-May 2026 public open house, the early August 2026 construction start and the August 31 2027 completion date, and for the Hannah Cleverly quote about a fighting chance.
https://www.thedailyworld.com/2026/06/01/westport-gets-birdseye-view-of-tsunami-tower-plans/
Oregon — the ban and the repeal
Oregon Legislature, House Bill 3309, 2019 regular session. The measure record for the repeal, signed June 25 2019.
https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2019R1/Measures/Overview/HB3309
DOGAMI Open-File Report O-03-05, development in Oregon's tsunami inundation zone, 2003. The state's own guide to the Senate Bill 379 prohibition, including the lines that evacuation is the preferred strategy for dealing with tsunami risk and that a local tsunami can come onshore in 10 to 30 minutes with the shaking as the only warning.
https://pubs.oregon.gov/dogami/ofr/O-03-05.pdf
Oregon Tsunami Clearinghouse, DOGAMI publications and GIS. Oregon's evacuation mapping, still maintained after the repeal.
https://www.oregon.gov/dogami/tsuclearinghouse/pages/pubs-gis.aspx
KGW, a giant step backwards: scientists say new tsunami zone building law puts Oregonians in danger.
https://www.kgw.com/article/news/a-giant-step-backwards-scientists-say-new-tsunami-zone-building-law-puts-oregonians-in-danger/283-f8a52a1d-e429-46f7-bc65-157a9d14c48c
Chris Goldfinger, Oregon State University, writing at Temblor: Oregon legislature turns its back on tsunamis, July 2 2019. The scientific objection, in the words of a researcher who studies the fault.
https://temblor.net/earthquake-insights/oregon-legislature-turns-its-back-on-tsunamis-8966/
Tillamook Headlight Herald, HB 3309 allows public structures to be built in tsunami zones, August 1 2019. Representative David Gomberg's response, included so both sides of the vote are represented.
https://www.tillamookheadlightherald.com/news_paid/hb-allows-public-structures-to-be-built-in-tsunami-zones/article_fb0940da-b3e6-11e9-8557-9bb658bdd0a2.html
Check your own address — free public tools
Washington DNR geology portal, tsunami hazard and evacuation mapping.
https://geologyportal.dnr.wa.gov/#tsu
NANOOS tsunami evacuation walk time modeling, hosted for Washington EMD.
https://nvs.nanoos.org/TsunamiEvac
Pacific Northwest Seismic Network, live earthquake monitoring for Washington and Oregon.
https://pnsn.org/
