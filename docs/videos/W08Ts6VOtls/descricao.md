The Doomsday Glacier is about to lose its brake. Thwaites is shedding 50 billion tons of ice a year, and the slab holding it back may break apart in 2026.

Thwaites Glacier in West Antarctica is the widest glacier on Earth - about 120 kilometers, roughly 80 miles, where it meets the sea - and it drains a basin larger than Florida. It already accounts for around 4 percent of global sea level rise. Its floating eastern ice shelf is not the flood risk. It is the brake. Robert Larter of the British Antarctic Survey says that brake is very likely to break up this year.

Here is what we get into: the retrograde slope that lets this glacier feed its own retreat, what the Icefin robot found after a team drilled 600 meters through the ice in 2023, why NASA's sea level fingerprint puts an extra 14 to 15 inches on the U.S. East Coast, and NOAA's projection of 10 to 12 inches of American sea level rise by 2050 - up to 100 high tide flood days a year around Norfolk, Virginia. We also cover the 2024 Science Advances study led by Mathieu Morlighem that ruled out the ice cliff domino scenario for this century. This is not a prediction that Thwaites collapses soon. The science says it does not.
