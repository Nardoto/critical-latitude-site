The seismic signal and the reclassification
USGS machine-readable event record confirming event us7000tbwb was solved as event type "landslide", magnitude 5.2, depth 0 km, origin 2026-08-26 at 02:52:10 UTC, 55 km northwest of Kodari
https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/query?eventid=us7000tbwb&format=geojson
USGS public event page, titled "M 5.2 Landslide - 55 km NW of Kodari, Nepal"
https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us7000tbwb/executive
USGS Landslide Hazards Program write-up: the roughly 100 km (62 mile) runout, the high-grade metamorphic bedrock (gneiss, quartzite, marble), the M5.2 initial failure and a secondary M4.2 about three hours later
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/landslide-hazards/science/2026-nepal-debris-avalanche-and-flash-flood
GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences independently solving the event at 5.2 to 5.7 at 02:52:23 UTC, and attributing it to break-off of a hanging glacier together with the underlying bedrock
https://www.gfz.de/en/press/news/details/extreme-sturzflut-in-china-und-nepal
Why automated processing first published the signal as a magnitude 4.4 earthquake: waveform shape, source depth and frequency content separate a prolonged shallow mass movement from a tectonic rupture
https://www.severe-weather.eu/global-weather/nepal-tibet-flood-ice-rock-avalanche-usgs-landslide-august-2026-rrc/
The revised mechanism: bedrock failed first
Dan Shugar's revision ("the glacier was only part of what failed... a large rock avalanche that took part of the glacier with it"), Jakob Steiner on the lower glacier tongue shearing off because the rock below failed, and Kristen Cook's climate-attribution caveat
https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-glacial-collapse-likely-caused-the-nepal-disaster-and-why-climate-change-will-make-these-events-more-likely/
Dave Petley, The Landslide Blog at Eos/AGU, 26 August 2026: the seismic signal is almost certainly the rock-ice avalanche and debris flow rather than a tectonic event, and there is no evidence this was a glacial lake outburst flood
https://eos.org/thelandslideblog/26-august-2026-nepal-and-tibet
Umesh Haritashya, University of Dayton, on permafrost acting as a natural cement in high mountain rock, and the Chamoli 2021 comparison at roughly 80 percent rock to 20 percent ice

https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-avalanche-was-live-on-drone-camera
