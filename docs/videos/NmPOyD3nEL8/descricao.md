Mount St. Helens: scientists watch five signs before this volcano erupts. 36 earthquakes in 26 days - here is how many of those five are happening now.

Between July first and July twenty-sixth, the Pacific Northwest Seismic Network located 36 earthquakes beneath Mount St. Helens. A normal month is about 22. So it is up, and not dramatically. The largest was magnitude 1.9. The smallest was magnitude minus 0.3 - a negative magnitude - and that number ends up mattering more than the count does. The scientists who monitor this mountain keep a published list of five things they expect to see before it erupts again. Today the score is zero out of five, and four of the five are things you can check yourself tonight for free.

We walk all five with the instrument attached to each one: earthquake depth, long-period events and drumbeat tremor, GPS deformation, the sulfur dioxide to carbon dioxide ratio, and steam blasts. Then the 1980 sequence, which ran the entire checklist in public - the March sixteenth earthquakes, the March twenty-seventh steam eruption, a bulge on the north flank moving two meters a day until it had shifted 450 feet, the magnitude 5.1 at 8:32 on May eighteenth, the largest landslide in recorded history, and 57 dead. USGS ranks Mount St. Helens second on the 2018 National Volcanic Threat Assessment at 235, ahead of Mount Rainier at 203, and calls it the most likely volcano in the lower 48 states to erupt. Recharge is real and the reservoir is filling. This is an explainer of what the monitoring shows, not a prediction that an eruption is imminent.
