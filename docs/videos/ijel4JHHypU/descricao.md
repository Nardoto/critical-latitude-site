The Nepal flood that tore through Rasuwa on August 26 was published as a magnitude 4.4 earthquake, and then the USGS took the earthquake back.

At 8:37 in the morning local time, seismometers picked up something north of Kathmandu and the automatic solution went out as a quake. What actually happened was a rock and ice avalanche off Langtang Lirung, a 23,700-foot Himalayan peak, and the debris flow that followed ran down the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli valleys for nearly 100 kilometers. It took out 41 bridges, 26 miles of road, more than 300 vehicles, and a string of hydropower plants sitting on the same river.
