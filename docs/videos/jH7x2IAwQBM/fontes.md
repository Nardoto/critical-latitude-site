Sources - Everything We Know About Krakatoa's Eruption
This document lists the sources behind the Critical Latitude video on Anak Krakatau, the 2018 Sunda Strait tsunami and the volcano's regrowth. The video is educational. It is not a prediction of an imminent collapse, tsunami or evacuation.
The 2018 collapse and the months before it
Walter et al., Nature Communications (2019). The reconstruction of the December 22nd, 2018 flank collapse, combining satellite radar, thermal observations and ground instruments. Source for the southwestern flank moving seaward for months, the eruptive material loading the slope, and the small earthquake recorded shortly before failure.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12284-5
GFZ German Research Centre for Geosciences, institutional account of the Walter et al. study. Plain-language summary of the pre-collapse signals and why they were identified retrospectively rather than as an operational advance warning.
https://www.gfz.de/en/press/news/details/early-warning-signals-heralded-fatal-collapse-of-krakatau-volcano
The seabed survey and the landslide deposit
Hunt et al., Nature Communications (2021). The August 2019 marine survey that mapped the failed material southwest of the island using bathymetry and seismic reflection. Source for the landslide blocks standing up to 70 m (about 230 feet) above the surrounding seabed, and for the continued deposition burying the evidence.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-22610-5
Regrowth, island shape and future scenarios
Meredew et al., Bulletin of Volcanology (2026). Reconstruction of Anak Krakatau's development through 2023 from historical records, satellite and drone data. Source for the comparison of pre- and post-collapse growth, the gentler post-collapse underwater slope on the southwestern side, and the conditional growth scenarios. Publication year 2026; last reconstructed year 2023.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00445-026-01974-w
Island identity, caldera and geography
NASA Earth Observatory, closeup of Anak Krakatau. Source for the island's setting inside the caldera left by the 1883 collapse, in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/closeup-of-anak-krakatau-5022/
NASA Earth Observatory, unrest at Anak Krakatau. Overview of the volcano's activity and the surrounding landscape.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/unrest-at-anak-krakatau-152157/
The dated present observation
B.M.K.G. (Indonesia's Meteorology, Climatology and Geophysical Agency), multi-sensor INATEWS monitoring bulletin, September 6th, 2026. Source for no sea level anomaly indicating a tsunami detected in the Sunda Strait through 7:30 in the morning local time on that date. Live instructions for this coast come from the Indonesian authorities responsible for it.
https://www.bmkg.go.id/artikel/hasil-pemantauan-multi-sensor-inatews-di-sekitar-selat-sunda-tidak-terdeteksi-anomali-muka-laut-dan-meteotsunami-di-selat-sunda
Tsunami mechanisms and safety guidance
National Weather Service, About Tsunamis. Source for tsunamis being generated by volcanic activity and landslides as well as earthquakes, and for the constraints on local warning time.
https://www.weather.gov/safety/tsunami-about
National Weather Service, Before a Tsunami. Source for learning evacuation routes in advance and identifying which authority issues warnings.
https://www.weather.gov/safety/tsunami-before
National Weather Service, During a Tsunami. Source for the natural warning signs, moving to high ground without waiting, and staying away until authorities say it is safe to return.
https://www.weather.gov/safety/tsunami-during
