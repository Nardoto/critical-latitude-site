### C001 — @SeanThorntonMD — 6 likes

> I was a boy in Tacoma in 1965. I was in bed. My bed slid on the wooden floor from wall to wall. The ceiling in the hallway had a 6 foot crack, but that was about it. As for the 2001 Nisqually quake, I was at work in Seattle in a 6 story 90 year old brick hospital.  My office was in the only un-retrofitted part of the original building. It lasted about 35 seconds, during which the elevator wall outside my office collapsed, the ceiling was coming down, and the stairwell was damaged, becoming unusable for months. My boss and I ran down the hall to the new EQ-safe building. Many may know of those metal strips in the floors of large public buildings. Well, those are there so allow different parts to move at different rates and in different directions during an EQ. When we got to the junction of the two buildings, the metal strip had fallen into a void as the buildings were separating and then hitting each other about every 2 seconds.  When the building would separate, we could see all the way to the ground 6 floors below.  We jumped across the 18 inch "chasm" as the EQ was winding down. My home had minor cracks in ceiling plaster. I have since had my home wrapped horizontally and vertically with metal tape, the city made me add steel reinforcement in walls during a big remodel, and had the house bolted to the foundation. I live 2.5 blocks from a depression in the road and waviness in the curbs that probably mark the line of the Seattle Fault. I'm expecting the next every-30-years EQ described here (called a Benioff zone quake) any time before I'm out of my house (hopefully 20+ years). I have solar panels and full home batteries, which can power my electric car,  I grow my own veggies. and I make my own alcohol (wine).  I'm as ready as I can be.

### C005 — @w7don — 1 likes

> I will never forget the 2001 Nisqually quake. I was at home, in an apartment on the third floor of a small four story building in North Seattle that was built in 1977. At first, my roommate and I noticed our computer monitors were shaking in an odd way. Within seconds, we realized what was happening. With a cat in one arm and a laptop in the other, I quickly sat down on the floor under the door jam of our front door. My roommate was sitting directly across from me. We could feel the building moving back and forth underneath us in a terrifying way. At one point, I thought the whole building was going to collapse. When the shaking finally ended, we immediately went outside and found lots of other dazed and confused neighbors doing the same thing. My cat grew restless, and the building seemed fine, so we went back in moments later.
