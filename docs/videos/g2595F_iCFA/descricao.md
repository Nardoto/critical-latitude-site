The Hayward Fault is not overdue. That word never came from the U.S. Geological Survey, and the numbers underneath it say something a good deal more uncomfortable.

Here is what is actually happening. The fault runs under more than 300 buildings and structures, straight under the University of California Berkeley football stadium, and it is bending a curb in the city of Hayward by about a fifth of an inch — five millimeters — every year. The USGS HayWired scenario, a magnitude 7.0 starting beneath Oakland, projects 800 deaths, roughly 18,000 injuries, more than $82 billion in damage, over 400 separate fires, and East Bay neighborhoods without water for six weeks.
