Sources — Critical Latitude, Vid 26: The Hayward Fault Is Not Overdue
This document lists every source behind the figures and quotations in the video. The video is educational. It is not a prediction that an earthquake is imminent.
The fault itself, and the 1868 earthquake
USGS Fact Sheet 2018-3052, "The Hayward Fault — Is it due for a repeat of the powerful 1868 earthquake?" (Brocher, Boatwright, Lienkaemper, Prentice, Schwartz and Bundock, 2018). The primary document for the video. Source for the magnitude 6.8 on October 21, 1868; the roughly 30 deaths; the 20 miles of surface rupture from Warm Springs in Fremont north to San Leandro; the creep rate of about a fifth of an inch (5 mm) per year; the creeping being confined to the top three miles with the fault locked below; creep and small earthquakes accounting for only about one third of the fault's total movement; the more than 300 buildings and structures sitting on the fault trace; the twelve major earthquakes in the past 1,900 years and the last six dated to 1134, 1317, 1475, 1629, 1725 and 1868; the line that few places have paid so dearly to have a fault named after them; and the quotation that "a powerful, damaging earthquake can be expected at any time."
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20183052
USGS featured story on the same fact sheet, plain-language version of the 1868 record and the current hazard.
https://www.usgs.gov/news/featured-story/hayward-fault-it-due-a-repeat-powerful-1868-earthquake
USGS event page for the 1868 Hayward earthquake, including the historical damage record and the period when this was the quake people meant by "the great San Francisco earthquake."
https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/events/1868calif
UC Berkeley Seismology Lab, Hayward Fault hazards and preparedness, including the fault running directly beneath California Memorial Stadium and the stadium's seismic retrofit.
https://earthquakes.berkeley.edu/hayward/hayward_hazards.html
The HayWired scenario — the damage ledger
USGS Fact Sheet 2018-3016, "The HayWired earthquake scenario — We can outsmart disaster" (2018). Source for the magnitude 7.0 hypothetical rupture beginning beneath Oakland, the roughly 52 miles of rupture, the 800 deaths, the injury count, the more than $82 billion in property and direct business interruption losses in 2016 dollars, the 77,000 households displaced by damage alone rising to 152,000 with utility outages, the more than 400 fires, and the East Bay water outages of six weeks to six months.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20183016
USGS Scientific Investigations Report 2017-5013 v.2, "The HayWired Earthquake Scenario — Engineering Implications." The full technical volume behind the fact sheet, including the people trapped in collapsed buildings and in stalled elevators.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20175013v2
SPUR summary of the HayWired scenario, an independent restatement of the same figures for the Bay Area planning audience.
https://www.spur.org/news/2018-04-23/haywired-earthquake-scenario-how-we-can-outsmart-disaster
The supercomputer simulation — why the inland side shakes harder
Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, "Hayward fault earthquake simulations increase fidelity of ground motions." Source for the magnitude 7.0 simulation led by seismologist Artie Rodgers with Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, the jump from about 0.5 to 1 Hz in earlier models up to 4 to 5 Hz in this one (a four- to eight-fold increase in resolved frequency), and the finding that the soft sedimentary rock of the East Bay hills amplifies shaking so that inland communities can shake harder than the flats. Published in Geophysical Research Letters.
https://www.llnl.gov/article/44096/hayward-fault-earthquake-simulations-increase-fidelity-ground-motions
LLNL follow-up work using the Sierra and exascale systems, resolving frequencies up to 10 Hz across 50 magnitude-7 scenarios. Context for how far the modeling has moved since the original paper.
https://www.llnl.gov/article/54316/simulations-predict-ground-motion-earthquakes-bay-areas-hayward-fault
Peer-reviewed record of the higher-resolution Hayward Fault ground-motion simulations, with site-response corrections.
https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1769117
The probabilities — 72 percent versus one in three
USGS Fact Sheet 2016-3020, "Earthquake Outlook for the San Francisco Bay Region 2014-2043" (Working Group on California Earthquake Probabilities, UCERF3). Source for the 72 percent chance of a magnitude 6.7 or greater somewhere in the San Francisco Bay region by 2043 — a regional figure covering every fault added together — and for the roughly one-in-three figure for the Hayward Fault on its own.
https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2016/3020/fs20163020.pdf
USGS map of known active geologic faults in the San Francisco Bay region, showing the per-fault probabilities that are routinely misquoted as Hayward-specific.
https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/map-known-active-geologic-faults-san-francisco-bay-region
Early warning, and the ongoing seismic record
USGS ShakeAlert, the West Coast earthquake early warning system. Source for how the alert detects the faster first wave ahead of the damaging wave, and for the estimated reduction in injuries if the system is fully operational and people drop, cover and hold on.
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/shakealert
USGS Latest Earthquakes map, the live catalog where small Hayward Fault events — including the magnitude 3.8 near San Leandro on August 13, 2026, and its routine downward revisions in the first hour — are published and updated.
https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/map/
The engineered pipe
Alameda County Water District, Alvarado-Niles Pipeline seismic improvements. The district's project record for replacing rigid pipe where it crosses the Hayward Fault trace with pipe designed to take fault offset and creep and keep carrying water.
https://www.acwd.org/594/Alvarado-Niles-Pipeline-Seismic-Improvem
KTVU FOX 2, Bay Area water agencies upgrading infrastructure in anticipation of a catastrophic earthquake, including the ball-joint pipe crossings built to move with the ground.
https://www.ktvu.com/news/bay-area-water-agencies-upgrading-infrastructure-in-anticipation-of-catastrophic-earthquake
EERI case study on East Bay Municipal Utility District seismic pipeline and tunnel work, including the Claremont Tunnel designed for fault offset plus creep.
https://mitigation.eeri.org/files/resources-for-success/00023.pdf
