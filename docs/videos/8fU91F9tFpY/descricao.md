Mount Rainier ranks 3rd on the USGS national volcano threat list. Yellowstone ranks 21st. Here is what the hazard maps actually show — and why children south of Seattle rehearse walking away from it every spring.

Rainier does not need to erupt to kill people. The rock inside it has been chemically rotted into weak, water-saturated clay by its own hydrothermal system, and USGS confirms at least one past lahar — the Electron Mudflow, now tree-ring dated to late summer 1507 — came with no known eruption at all. They have a name for that scenario: a "no-notice" lahar. More than 90,000 people live where a large lahar can reach them, and Orting could be hit as little as 40 minutes after the warning sounds.
